# 02 — The Deep Dive: How the World Teaches Dance

The synthesis of a research sweep across online platforms, formal grading syllabi, master
teachers, African dance instruction, retention data, and the street-dance transmission
culture. This is the evidence base the curriculum (Docs 03–04) and platform (Doc 06) are
built on. Sources are named throughout; the raw findings live in this doc so we never have
to re-research the basics.

---

## 1. The online platforms (what exists, what wins, what fails)

### The benchmark: STEEZY Studio
- 600–800+ classes across Hip Hop, Ballet, Contemporary, K-Pop, House, Breaking, Popping,
  Whacking, Krump, Locking, Heels, and more. ~$19.99/mo or ~$99/yr.
- The most-copied player UX in the industry: **front/back camera toggle, mirroring,
  tempo control, section looping** — the table stakes any new platform must meet.
- Its key structural idea: **Classes vs. Programs.** Classes are one-off choreography;
  Programs are multi-week curricula in expert-prescribed order (e.g., Carlo Darang's
  Beginner Urban Program starting with grooves and rhythm before any choreography).
- Its recurring failures (from app-store/Trustpilot review mining): **music licensing**
  (tracks stripped/replaced — users' #1 content complaint), app bugs, billing surprises.

### The rest of the field
| Platform | Niche | Notable |
|---|---|---|
| CLI Studios (~$199/yr) | Studio/commercial styles | 1,000+ classes, celebrity faculty, weekly **live** classes — the "school vs. library" differentiator |
| DancePlug ($20/mo, $99/yr) | Multi-style | Multiple camera angles + tempos per video; bundles audition listings (industry-hub retention) |
| Broche Ballet | Adult ballet technique | 7,200+ classes, 50+ programs incl. "Journey to Pointe" (637 videos); engineering-style mechanical breakdowns — the depth standard |
| Ballet Beautiful / Lazy Dancer Tips | Ballet-as-fitness | Same style, different promise — a platform must pick its promise per style |
| Passion4Dancing ($167/yr) | Ballroom/Latin | 500+ lessons, 21 styles |
| NYC Mambo (Eddie Torres Jr.) | Salsa On2 | Shines + partnerwork + monthly live Q&As with musicians — lineage as product |
| Addicted2Salsa | Free salsa | 382K YouTube subs; the free top-of-funnel model |
| Kukuwa Fitness ($14.99/mo) | African dance cardio | Moves cued by country of origin; continent-wide styles |
| African Dance Dictionary | Afro dance | **The most platform-ready Afro model**: searchable named-move dictionary + 14-day beginner program + weekly live Zoom feedback |
| VincaniTV | Breaking (YouTube) | The reference free breaking instruction ecosystem (full detail in Doc 03) |

**Industry price point:** ~$99/year with a 7-day trial is the de facto standard.

### The retention reality every platform faces
- Fitness/learning apps: **day-30 retention 3–4%**; ~77% never return after day 1; median
  70% gone within 100 days.
- The two levers with hard numbers behind them: **week-one activation** (users who don't
  complete 3 sessions in week one churn at 4–5x the rate) and **annual billing** (33%
  retained vs 17% monthly).
- Dance studios (physical) hold ~70% year-over-year retention — the gap between "video
  library" and "school" is community, feedback, and milestones. **68% of students who
  perform in a recital re-enroll.** This is why Doc 05's Seasons end in performance events.

## 2. The formal syllabi (what "structured progression" looks like when it's done seriously)

These systems have graded millions of dancers over a century; we steal their architecture.

- **RAD ballet (Royal Academy of Dance)**: Pre-Primary → Primary → Grades 1–8, plus a
  parallel **vocational track** (Intermediate Foundation → Advanced 2). Pointe work is
  **gated on teacher-assessed physical readiness**, not enthusiasm. → *We copy: dual-track
  (recreational/serious) and readiness gates for injury-risk skills (pointe : ballet ::
  power moves : breaking).*
- **Vaganova method**: strict 8-year sequence; steps withheld until prerequisite strength
  and placement exist ("no room for bad habits"); the *sequencing itself is the
  curriculum*. → *We copy: hard prerequisites in the skill graph.*
- **ISTD**: graded exams AND **medal tests** (Bronze → Silver → Gold → Gold Star) across
  ballroom, Latin, tap — and notably already street dance. → *We copy: the medal ladder as
  a motivational overlay on the graph.*
- **Ballroom chains (Arthur Murray / Fred Astaire / DVIDA)**: Bronze/Silver/Gold where
  **level = allowed vocabulary set** (Bronze events permit only Bronze figures). Crucially,
  the same figure sits at *different* levels in different syllabi — proof that **levels are
  conventions, not physics**. Their retention tripod: private lessons + group classes +
  practice parties. → *We copy: continuous skill graph underneath, discrete badge labels on
  top; and the tripod maps to solo practice + community sessions + social events.*
- **Salsa**: Eddie Torres created the first mambo syllabus in 1972 and named 500+ steps —
  the named-move dictionary approach. Pedagogy consensus: start On1, meet On2 later.
  LA's Movers & Shakers had to invent **levels 1.5 and 1.75** because adult progression is
  continuous — more evidence for graph-under-badges.
- **Tango**: two coexisting philosophies — traditional/improvisational ("steps are raw
  material") vs. structured (Tango Space's L1–L4 with a defined 12-week beginner
  vocabulary: walk, embrace, cross, ochos, giro). The embrace/connection is universally
  described as unlearnable from video alone — an honest limit we design around (solo
  technique + timing online; partnered feel via real-world bridges).
- **Street styles**: historically transmitted through **cyphers, battles, sessions,
  mentorship** — not exams; self-teaching by observation is culturally native (good news
  for us). Institutions are leveling it anyway (ISTD street dance medals, UCLA's summer
  institute, STEEZY programs). The field's own standard: instruction must include
  **authentic content and cultural context**, or it reads as extractive. → *We copy:
  foundation → freestyle → cypher/battle-readiness as the progression frame, with history
  modules as first-class curriculum.*

## 3. African dance instruction (the styles you asked for specifically)

### Traditional West African (Guinea/Mali, djembe-accompanied)
- Master lineage teachers: **Youssouf Koumbassa** and **Mouminatou Camara** (both ex-Les
  Ballets Africains) — the pre-eminent diaspora teachers; classes at Cumbe (Brooklyn),
  Ailey Extension, plus annual immersions in Guinea.
- The **canonical 90-minute class format** is remarkably consistent worldwide: drum-connected
  warm-up (~15 min) → movement breakdown (~15 min) → **high-energy lines across the floor
  toward the drummers** → closing community circle; call-and-response songs; explicit
  teaching of *which drum rhythm accompanies which dance* (dundunba, kuku, lamban…) and
  the **drum "break"** that cues transitions.
- Online, this tradition is badly underserved (workshop listings, not curricula) — because
  the pedagogy depends on live drum–dancer dialogue. Our design answer: multi-track rhythm
  audio, break-cue ear training, and rhythm-name literacy as core lessons (Doc 04).

### Afrobeats / Afrodance (Ghana & Nigeria)
- **DWP Academy** (Accra, co-founded by Dancegod Lloyd) is the world's flagship Afrodance
  school; its orbit created globally adopted named moves — Incredible Zigi ("Pilolo,"
  "Ena"), Endurance ("Oya").
- The pedagogy is organized around **named moves** (Shaku Shaku, Zanku/legwork, Gbese,
  Network…): learn 3–5 moves, then combine. The African Dance Dictionary platform proves
  the model: move dictionary + short program + weekly live feedback.

### Southern & Central Africa
- **Pantsula** (SA township footwork, apartheid-era resistance storytelling), **gumboot**
  (mine-born percussive form), **amapiano** (TikTok-native step culture), **Congolese
  ndombolo** (best online teacher: CiiCii's Congolese Dance Tutorials — loketo hip work,
  kisanola, makolongulu). No dominant structured platform exists for any of these — all
  creator-led. This is the largest open content gap found in the entire research sweep.

### The respect architecture (non-negotiable design requirements)
From practitioner discourse (Angel Kaba's "Teaching African Dance as a Non-African,"
LREI's "Authenticity Without Appropriation," academic work on commodification):
1. Credit culture and lineage on-screen (who created the move, where, in what tradition).
2. Partner with and pay originator/African-based instructors (DWP model) — revenue share,
   not re-teaching their material.
3. History and meaning modules ship *with* the steps, not as optional extras.
4. Permission/blessing from tradition-bearers where lineage matters (West African forms).
5. Frame street/Afro progression as freestyle/cypher readiness, never exam passing.

## 4. Why adults quit — and what keeps them dancing

**Quit causes (studios + scenes):** scheduling conflicts (45% of dropouts), shame at
starting over / can't-reach-former-level beliefs, no personal feedback or recognition,
scene aggravations (cliquishness, late events). **App quit cause:** week one with < 3
sessions.

**Stay causes:**
- The Dance Motivation Inventory's strongest factor is **mood enhancement**, followed by
  socializing, fitness, mastery, escapism — recreational adults optimize for connection +
  stress relief; serious learners for mastery. One engagement strategy won't fit both →
  our dual-track design.
- **Performance milestones** (68% re-enroll after performing).
- **Social dance nights / cyphers** — the community *is* the retention mechanism in every
  scene: milongas for tango, socials for salsa, cyphers and battles for street styles.
  The recognized street-dance confidence ladder: freestyle alone → familiar cypher →
  unfamiliar cypher → battle. Doc 05's Seasons and relatedness layer implement exactly
  this ladder.

## 5. The cross-style commonalities (the shared foundation layer)

Five clusters recur in every tradition studied — these become the "Common Core" every
style pack shares (Doc 04):

1. **Rhythm & musicality** — the universal substrate: counts, instrument-following,
   phrasing, breaks. Hip hop's groove training, salsa's clave/conga ear, West African
   drum-break cues, ballroom's "hear, feel, respond," tango's phrasing — one trainable
   skill with different accents.
2. **Grooves/bounce** — the shared motor foundation of hip hop, Afrobeats, amapiano,
   ndombolo, house, dancehall.
3. **Body isolations** (head, shoulders, ribs, hips) — recur in hip hop, afro styles,
   salsa styling ("Cuban motion"), jazz.
4. **Partner connection** (frame/embrace, weight-transfer communication, active following)
   — shared by salsa, bachata, tango, ballroom; the weakest link for pure video learning;
   trained online via solo shines, timing drills, and follower-technique work, completed
   in the real world.
5. **Alignment, balance, turnout** (ballet's gift to everyone) — balance as a trainable
   skill of micro-adjustments; turnout from the hip as an active muscular action; the
   injury-safety knowledge base all styles borrow.

## 6. The ten design implications (carried into Docs 03–07)

1. Player table stakes: front/back view, mirror, tempo, A/B loop, angles — then exceed
   them with 3D (Doc 06).
2. Solve music licensing on day one (the industry's #1 content complaint) — Doc 06 §6.
3. Two content atoms everywhere: the **named move (with provenance)** and the **leveled
   program with readiness gates**. The platform = cross-style move dictionary + per-style
   programs on one graph engine.
4. Continuous skill graph underneath, discrete badges on top (levels are conventions).
5. Readiness gates for injury-risk skills (pointe, dips, power moves).
6. Respect architecture for African/street content (credit, revenue share, context).
7. Retention engines by track: performance milestones, social bridges, battle/cypher
   ladders — plus week-one activation design and annual billing.
8. Live/feedback layer separates "school" from "library" (weekly live or async coach
   review, à la African Dance Dictionary and CLI).
9. Shared foundation curriculum (musicality, grooves, isolations, alignment, connection
   prep) feeding every style track.
10. Free top-of-funnel (YouTube-style open content) feeding the structured paid system —
    the acquisition model every successful platform in this space uses.
