# 04 — Every Dance, One Engine: The Multi-Style Curriculum Framework

Breaking (Doc 03) is Mountain One. This document defines the **repeatable template** that
turns any dance on earth into a curriculum pack on the same engine — and seeds it with the
styles in the vision: hip hop, salsa, ballet, tango, and the African dances. The template
is distilled from the systems that have actually graded millions of dancers (RAD, Vaganova,
ISTD, ballroom Bronze/Silver/Gold, Eddie Torres's salsa syllabus, Tango Space's levels,
STEEZY's programs, DWP/African Dance Dictionary's named-move model — Doc 02).

---

## 1. The Common Core (trained once, used by every style)

Research found five skill clusters recurring in every tradition. They form a shared
foundation track that every style pack plugs into — progress here carries across styles,
which is itself a motivation mechanic (starting salsa after breaking, you're *not* a
beginner):

| Core module | What it trains | Feeds |
|---|---|---|
| **Musicality I–III** | counting, finding the 1, instrument-following (kick/snare/clave/conga/dundun), phrasing, breaks, dancing *to* vs *near* music | everything |
| **Grooves & bounce** | up/down grooves, rock, weight shifts, riding a beat in the body | hip hop, breaking toprock, Afrobeats, house, amapiano, ndombolo |
| **Isolations** | head, shoulders, ribs, hips — separated and layered | hip hop, afro styles, salsa styling (Cuban motion), jazz |
| **Alignment & balance** | stacked posture, micro-adjustment balance skill, hip-sourced turnout, core engagement | ballet, all spins/turns, injury safety everywhere |
| **Connection prep** (solo) | frame/embrace posture, weight-transfer clarity, lead-from-center, active-following drills | salsa, bachata, tango, ballroom |

Plus the **body preparation spine** (strength/mobility from Doc 03 §6) which is shared
infrastructure: wrists/shoulders/core for floor styles, ankles/feet/turnout for ballet,
hips everywhere.

## 2. The style-pack template (how any dance becomes a pack)

Every pack ships the same seven components on the skill-graph engine (Doc 06 §5):

1. **Move dictionary with provenance** — every named move a node: who created/carries it,
   where it comes from, what music it lives in. (Eddie Torres named 500+ salsa steps;
   Afrobeats organizes itself around named moves like Pilolo and Zanku; ballroom syllabi
   are figure lists. The named move is the universal content atom.)
2. **Leveled path with readiness gates** — a mostly-linear default route through the graph
   (Foundation → Vocabulary → Flow/Combination → Style/Expression → Scene-Ready), with
   hard gates on injury-risk skills, RAD-pointe-style (power moves, multiple turns, dips,
   pointe).
3. **Musicality module for that style's music** — the specific rhythms, instruments, song
   structures, and break cues (clave for salsa, dundun breaks for Guinean dance, the 808
   and amapiano log drum, tango phrasing).
4. **Culture & history module** — first-class lessons, not footnotes: where the dance was
   born, who its pioneers are, what its gatherings look like, its etiquette (cypher,
   milonga, social, drum circle).
5. **Drill library** — the repeatable practice atoms the session generator interleaves
   (SRS review, Doc 05 Law 8).
6. **Freestyle/social prompts** — style-appropriate improvisation and combination
   training, because the end-state of every style is dancing, not reciting.
7. **Scene bridge** — the graduated real-world ladder for that style (Doc 02 §4):
   freestyle alone → filmed clip → familiar group → public social/cypher/milonga →
   battle/performance.

**Levels are conventions, not physics** (the same ballroom figure sits at different levels
in different syllabi): the graph holds the truth (prerequisites), the levels are the
motivational display layer.

## 3. Seeded style packs

### 3.1 Hip Hop (Pack 2 — closest to breaking, shares the most Core)
- Source systems: STEEZY's Beginner Urban Program ordering (grooves → rhythm → foundation
  steps → choreography), the party-dance canon, UCLA-style culture framing.
- Foundation vocabulary: bounce/rock grooves; **party moves & social dances** (running man,
  roger rabbit, cabbage patch, reject, dougie, smurf, prep, steve martin, humpty…each a
  provenance-tagged node); levels of groove (chest/arm/step layering).
- Progression: Grooves → Foundation steps (era by era: 80s/90s/2000s) → Layering &
  texture → Freestyle frameworks (concept-based improv) → Choreography & cypher.
- Musicality: boom-bap vs trap vs funk; riding the pocket; hitting vs flowing.

### 3.2 Salsa (Pack 3 — first partner style)
- Source systems: Eddie Torres On2 syllabus & named-step catalog; On1-first consensus
  pedagogy (Doc 02); Movers & Shakers' continuous leveling; Addicted2Salsa/Dance Dojo
  free pedagogy.
- Solo-first design (our platform is solo-first): **shines** track (basic, suzy q,
  flares…), Cuban motion, styling, and clave/conga ear training carry a solo learner to
  social-ready; partnerwork nodes (cross-body lead, right turn, copa…) unlock as
  "requires partner" flagged nodes with connection-prep drills done solo.
- Progression: On1 basic & timing → Shines I → Partnerwork I (CBL family) → Turns &
  spotting → Shines II + styling → On2 timing conversion → Social-floor craft (floorcraft,
  musicality across a song, dancing small).
- Scene bridge: studio social nights (the beginner-safe on-ramp the research identified).

### 3.3 Ballet (Pack 4 — the technique spine)
- Source systems: RAD grade structure (dual recreational/vocational tracks), Vaganova
  sequencing discipline, Broche Ballet's adult-focused mechanical depth (the proof adults
  can learn real technique online).
- Adult-online promise: **technique-education, not ballet-as-fitness** (Doc 02's split) —
  barre fundamentals, alignment, turnout as active hip action, adagio balance, pirouette
  prep with hard readiness gates; pointe explicitly out of scope without in-person
  assessment (honest limit, stated in-app).
- Ballet doubles as the platform's **alignment/balance/control gym** for every other style.

### 3.4 Argentine Tango (Pack 5 — the connection art)
- Source systems: Tango Space L1–L4 vocabulary (walk, posture, embrace, cross, ochos,
  giro), traditional improvisation-first philosophy, milonga etiquette curriculum.
- Honest-limit design: the embrace "cannot be taught by showing" — online we train
  walking quality, posture, musicality (phrasing, orchestras), solo technique
  (ochos/pivots/balance), floorcraft rules, códigos (cabeceo etc.); the scene bridge
  (attend a practica by week N of the season) is *built into the path*, not optional.

### 3.5 African Dances (Pack 6+ — the largest content gap found, and a vision priority)
Split into distinct packs, never one "African dance" blob:
- **West African (Guinean/Malian djembe tradition)**: modeled on the canonical global
  class format — drum-connected warm-up, movement breakdown, lines toward the drummers,
  community circle. Curriculum = rhythm-name literacy (dundunba, kuku, lamban, soli…),
  which dance belongs to which rhythm and occasion, **drum-break ear training** (the break
  cues every transition), then the movement vocabulary per rhythm. Built with lineage
  teachers (the Koumbassa/Camara school of pedagogy) as paid consultants/instructors.
- **Afrobeats/Afrodance (Ghana/Nigeria)**: the named-move dictionary model proven by
  African Dance Dictionary and DWP Academy — Shaku Shaku, Zanku/legwork, Pilolo, Gbese,
  Network…, each credited to its creator; 3–5 moves → combos → freestyle.
- **South & Central African styles**: amapiano steps, pantsula footwork (with its
  resistance history), gumboot rhythm work, Congolese ndombolo (loketo hip work) — all
  currently creator-led with no structured platform anywhere: partnering with those
  creators (CiiCii-style teachers) is both the right thing and an open market.
- **Respect architecture applies in full** (Doc 02 §3): on-screen provenance, originator
  revenue share, history modules shipped with the steps, African-based instructors
  captured and credited (mocap performers are named artists, not anonymous data).

## 4. Cross-style progression & the polymath path

- **Shared Core credit**: Core modules completed in any style auto-credit everywhere —
  the breaking-trained dancer enters hip hop at Vocabulary level, salsa with musicality
  pre-cleared. Visible transfer = the motivation to start style #2.
- **The polymath arc** (the personal vision): Year 1 = Breaking + Common Core; Year 2 =
  + Hip Hop and Salsa (light packs alongside continued breaking); Year 3+ = Ballet
  (technique spine), Tango, African packs — sequenced so each new style lands on
  transferred foundation, and the daily session generator (Doc 05 §3) can mix styles in
  one session ("breaking review + salsa shines + ballet balance block").
- **One identity, many dances**: the profile is a single dancer identity with per-style
  trees lit up — the "master of dance itself" made visible.
